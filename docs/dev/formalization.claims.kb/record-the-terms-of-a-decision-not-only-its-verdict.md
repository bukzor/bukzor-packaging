# Record the Terms of a Decision, Not Only Its Verdict

Carrier: a decision record -- an index row saying what was decided. Law:

> A verdict without its terms carves **no half-plane**, so a ledger that records
> only verdicts can never elicit its own exchange rate no matter how many
> decisions it makes. The terms are available at decision time and at no later
> time.

The second sentence is why this is not a filing preference. A reconstruction
made when the rate is finally wanted is fitted to the verdict already reached --
it will produce numbers consistent with what was decided, which is precisely the
data `ELICIT` cannot use. The estimate must be captured while it is still
capable of being wrong.

## What "the terms" means

Both sides of whatever inequality was applied, as estimated *then*, plus a
`--` and a reason where a side was never estimated. The admission is a real
record and discharges the obligation: a decision with an unestimated term is
degenerate, it constrains no rate, and saying so is the finding rather than a
gap in the paperwork.

## The obligation has to be a check

It comes due at the instant of the decision and never again, so a prose
convention cannot enforce it -- by the time anyone reads the convention, the
moment is gone. The check compares the index's settled decisions against its
recorded terms and fails on the difference.

## Smallest instance

Four settled decisions in the ledger this collection came from. Exactly one has
terms anybody can still recover, because it was the case where a term was
literally zero. The other three were made by weighing something against
something and writing down only the outcome, and the feasible exchange rate
after all four is still the whole positive ray -- the same as before any
decision was made.

## What would kill it

A domain where the terms are recomputable from durable artifacts at any later
time. Then the record is redundant with the artifacts and the reconstruction is
not fitted, because it does not consult the verdict.
