# A Claim Pays Off Only Where It Is Read

Carrier: a filed claim and the occasions on which the decision it governs comes
up. Law:

> The retention payoff is P(consulted at the decision) × value, and the first
> factor is a property of **where the claim is filed**, not of how good it is.
> Filing by theory makes the argument coherent for the author. Filing by
> occasion makes the claim arrive for the reader. A ledger needs both indices
> and usually builds one.

The two orders genuinely conflict: the theory poset groups by shared vocabulary,
and a reader arriving with a decision does not know which vocabulary it needs.
So the poset cannot be the retrieval index, and a claim reachable only through
it is a claim reachable only by someone already reading the theory.

## Smallest instance

This collection's parent ledger. Fifty-odd claims in eleven theories, and the
realistic read -- an agent under context pressure, opening the subject for the
first time -- is the entry point and nothing else. Which is exactly why that
entry point carries the whole picture on one page: **retrieval was being obeyed
before it was stated**, and the one-page summary is the index, not a courtesy.

The general mechanism is a trigger-shaped file whose *name* states the occasion
that requires it -- `before/<action>.md` -- so that matching the situation
against a directory listing is the whole lookup.

## The consequence for filing

Every claim should be answerable from the occasion that needs it. In practice
that means an occasion index maintained alongside the poset, and it means a
claim whose occasion cannot be named is a claim nobody will retrieve -- which is
`PAYOFF`'s "vocabulary" verdict reached from the other side.

## What would kill it

A reader for whom loading everything is free. If the entire ledger fits in
context at no cost, arrival is solved and the poset is the only index needed.
The claim is really about a *budgeted* reader, and it strengthens as the ledger
grows.
