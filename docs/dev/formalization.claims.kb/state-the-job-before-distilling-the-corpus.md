# State the Job Before Distilling the Corpus

Carrier: a theory, the corpus it is distilled from, and its job -- the
decisions it must govern, for whom. Law:

> Distillation is bounded by the corpus: a term enters the theory only where
> the corpus has paid it. The job is not in the corpus, so **coverage cannot
> be graded bottom-up** -- it is graded against a requirements statement
> derived from the job, written apart from the distillation it grades.

`PAYOFF`'s bar cannot see this failure: it grades filed structures one at a
time, and a missing term files nothing to grade. `CARRIER` cannot either --
the objects can be right while a cost the job prices has simply never been
paid in the data. The declined alternative is auditing harder, and it
converges: every audit of what exists finds defects in what exists.

## Smallest instance

The parent ledger. Bottom-up it graded well -- checks red for honest reasons,
defeaters filed, kills recorded -- while the costliest term of its own
subject, the obligations a published package creates, appeared in no claim,
because the corpus is solo-authored and had never paid one. Three rounds of
review found in-file defects instead; the term entered the ledger
(`CONTRACT`) only after the job was stated and graded against:
`../../../requirements.md`, R1.

## What would kill it

A corpus that has already paid every cost the job prices -- then distillation
reaches coverage and the requirements statement is redundant. The cheaper
failure to watch for: a requirements pass that never finds a gap the checks
missed is ceremony, and should be retired by its own ledger's rules.
