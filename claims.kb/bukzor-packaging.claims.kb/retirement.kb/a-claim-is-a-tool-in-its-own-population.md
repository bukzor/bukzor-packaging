# A Claim Is a Tool in Its Own Population

Carrier: the text of this ledger, as members of the population its own rules
range over. Law:

> A claim has a maintenance toll -- read cost × encounters -- and a marginal
> benefit -- the decisions it changes that no surviving claim would change. So
> `PRUNE` applies to it, at the same gate and on the same list.

No exemption is available. None of the rules here mentions a tool, a script, or a
`~/bin`; they range over artifacts with encounters and benefits, and prose has
both. The ledger acquired a fixed point the moment `PRUNE` was filed, and it is
not "every claim forever" any more than it is "every tool forever".

## Where the pressure lands, which is not where the instinct says

`TOLL` charges by encounter, so the toll follows **namespace heat**. The entry
point and every maintenance guide are read at the start of every session that
touches the subject; a leaf case study is read when someone goes looking for it,
perhaps once. So the expensive text is the *summary*, and a paragraph of exhibit
detail three directories down is nearly free.

That inverts the usual editing instinct, which trims the details and protects the
overview. The correct move is the reverse: the entry point runs a fixed budget, a
new finding earns its line there by displacing one, and detail moves **down**
rather than out. Deleting a case study to save space is the same error as
retiring cold code -- a real deletion whose win rounds to zero.

## Smallest instance

The instruction to keep a maintenance guide short, which reads like a style
preference and is this claim. A guide is loaded before any of its value arrives,
so its tokens are paid on every encounter and refunded only on the encounters
where a decision actually turns. That is a toll-versus-marginal-benefit argument
with the words removed, and stating it as taste hid the fact that it has a
threshold and applies unevenly across the ledger.

## What would kill it

Text with no encounter cost -- retrieval that loads the one claim a decision
needs instead of the file it happens to live in. The toll would fall toward zero
for everything not retrieved, the gate would stop firing on prose, and what
survives is an argument about *finding* claims rather than about keeping them.
That is a different theory and it is not this one.
